89% of our sales came from 3 accounts. Here's what that taught me.

Three of the nine ad accounts we run produced 89% of all the sales Meta attributes to our ads. They took 72% of the spend. The gap between those two numbers is the whole lesson.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

When I broke our EGP 10.9 million down by account, one fact jumped out: three accounts produced almost nine out of every ten pounds of attributed sales.

89%of attributed sales from 3 accounts
72%of total spend in those 3 accounts
3.8Xtheir combined return
1.1Xthe other accounts combined

Meta Ads Manager, lifetime data from October 2023 to November 2026 across 9 ad accounts. Brand names hidden.

Who the three are

  • A perfume brand: about EGP 4.65 million in attributed sales from EGP 1.29 million of spend.
  • A laptop store: about EGP 2.81 million from EGP 681,000.
  • An online store: about EGP 2.30 million from EGP 616,000.

Together they spent about EGP 2.59 million and brought about EGP 9.76 million, a combined return of roughly 3.8X. The remaining accounts spent about EGP 1.02 million and show about EGP 1.15 million in attributed sales, roughly 1.1X.

Why the rest look weak, and why that's misleading

That 1.1X looks bad until you see what's inside it. Most of the money in the "other" group went to brands that sell through messages and branches: a perfume retail chain running branch campaigns and a retail brand whose sales happen in its stores. Their campaigns were built to start conversations, not website purchases, so Ads Manager barely credits them with sales. Judging them by purchase return is like judging a shop by its online orders when its door is always busy.

So the honest split isn't "good accounts and bad accounts". It's "accounts where the sale happens on a website" and "accounts where the sale happens somewhere Meta can't see".

What the top three have in common

Looking across the three accounts that carry the total, a few things stand out:

  • A product people already want. Perfumes, laptops and the online store's catalog sell because there's real demand, not because ads create it from nothing.
  • A website that converts. In all three, people can go from an ad to a finished order quickly.
  • Consistent budgets over a long time. These accounts spent steadily, month after month, which gives Meta enough purchase data to keep improving.
  • Order values that leave room. An average order of about EGP 1,450 for the perfume brand and about EGP 4,600 for the laptop store leaves enough margin to pay for ads and still profit.

What concentration teaches an agency

For me, a number like 89% is a reminder to be honest with every brand. Some brands are ready to scale with ads tomorrow. Others first need a better offer, a better product page, a faster inbox or simply more time. Telling them the truth early is better than taking a budget and hoping.

It's also a reminder about risk. An agency that depends on a few accounts has to keep earning them every month, and has to keep building the next ones.

What it means for your brand

If your account looks like the "other" group, ask two questions. First: where does your sale actually happen? If it's in the inbox or in a branch, measure that, not website ROAS. Second: if it's on your website, what's stopping it? Usually it's one of the four things above.
