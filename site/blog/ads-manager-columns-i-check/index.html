The Ads Manager columns I actually check, and the ones I ignore

Ads Manager can show hundreds of metrics. Most decisions need about fifteen. Here's my column setup, from spend to purchases, and why each one is there.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

The default columns in Ads Manager are built for everyone, which means they're built for no one in particular. For the cash-on-delivery stores we run, I use one saved custom set of columns and read it the same way every time: from left to right, following the buyer's path from seeing the ad to ordering.

1. Delivery: is the ad being shown?

  • Amount spent.
  • Impressions and reach.
  • Frequency: how many times the average person saw the ad. Rising frequency with falling clicks is often the first sign of a tired creative.
  • CPM, the cost of 1,000 impressions. It tells me how expensive the audience is, not whether the ad works.

2. Attention: do people stop?

  • Hook rate: 3-second video plays divided by impressions. It's a custom metric you create once in Ads Manager and reuse.
  • Hold rate: ThruPlays divided by 3-second plays. Do the people who stopped keep watching?

3. Clicks: do they want more?

  • Link clicks, link click-through rate and cost per link click.
  • Landing page views. If they're far below link clicks, people are leaving before the page loads, which usually means the page is too slow.

4. Intent: are they serious?

  • Adds to cart and cost per add to cart.
  • Checkouts initiated. For many cash-on-delivery order forms, this is the moment the form is opened or started.

5. Results: does it pay?

  • Purchases and cost per purchase.
  • Purchase conversion value and purchase ROAS.
  • For messaging campaigns: messaging conversations started and cost per messaging conversation started.

6. Context: how should I read the row?

  • Attribution setting. Results differ between settings, so compare like with like.
  • Delivery status. An ad set that's still learning shouldn't be judged as harshly as a settled one.
  • Budget. A result only means something next to what it cost to get.

The breakdowns I check before changing anything

  • By day: is it a trend or one bad day?
  • By placement and platform: is one placement taking budget without results?
  • By age and gender: who is actually buying?
  • By region: which governorates order. In cash on delivery, it's worth comparing that with where orders are actually delivered.
  • By time of day: when people buy, which also tells the team when to be ready for messages.

What I mostly ignore

  • Post engagement, likes and reactions. Nice to see, rarely decisive.
  • The relevance diagnostics, Meta's quality, engagement and conversion rankings. I only look at them when an ad is underperforming and I want clues about why.

What no column shows

Ads Manager counts orders placed, not orders delivered. Next to the columns, keep a simple sheet per campaign with confirmed and delivered orders. In cash-on-delivery stores, that's the only way to know the real cost per order. I'll go deeper on this in CPA, ROAS and delivered orders.

These columns feed the decisions I describe in when to kill an ad and weekly creative testing. And none of them mean anything if tracking is broken, which is why the Pixel and Conversions API come first, as I explain in Pixel and Conversions API for COD.
