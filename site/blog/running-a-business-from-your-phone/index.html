Running a business from your phone: the setup we build for store owners

For many store owners, the phone is the office. So the systems we build report to the phone first: every order, every sale and every day's numbers, right where the owner already looks.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Most store owners I work with don't spend their day at a desk. They're at a branch, at the warehouse, with a supplier or on the road. If finding out how the business is doing requires a laptop, they find out late, usually after a problem has already cost money.

So when we build a store or a system, one of the first questions is simple: what does the owner need to see on the phone, and when?

Three layers: alerts, summaries and a dashboard

1. Instant alerts for anything that needs action now. A new online order, or a sale at a branch. These reach Telegram within seconds, in the hands of the people who act on them. I explain what a useful alert contains in why every order should ping your phone.

2. A daily summary for decisions. In the shop systems we've built for businesses with branches, a report arrives on Telegram at the end of every day, so the owner knows how each branch did without calling anyone. I describe those systems in what an ERP for a perfume business must do.

3. A dashboard for when you want to dig deeper. Alerts and summaries answer "what happened". A dashboard answers "why": which products sell, which days are slow, and where orders come from.

What we asked for when we built our orders dashboard

For the online stores we run, we built an orders dashboard, and the brief was that it should feel like a phone app, not a website squeezed onto a small screen:

  • A notification and a sound for every new order.
  • New orders highlighted until someone opens them, so nothing gets missed.
  • Roles, so staff see what they need and the owner sees everything.
  • Orders that can be edited from the phone: remove a product, add one, change a quantity.
  • Reports with date ranges and comparisons, the best-selling products, and where orders come from by governorate.

Messages and ads, from the same phone

Orders are only half of it. For most of the brands we work with, the sale starts in a message or a comment.

  • Meta Business Suite puts Facebook and Instagram messages and comments in one inbox on the phone.
  • WhatsApp Business handles confirmations, with quick replies and labels to keep track of who has confirmed.
  • The Ads Manager app is enough to check spend and results, or to pause something urgent. Bigger changes still deserve a computer and a calm hour.

Rules that keep the phone useful, not noisy

  • Alerts only for things that need action. Everything else belongs in the daily summary. Too many pings and people stop reading them.
  • Separate groups for separate jobs. Confirmation, warehouse and owner summaries don't need to be the same chat.
  • Customer data only for the people who need it. Phone numbers and addresses are private.
  • Personal logins, never shared passwords. Every person gets their own access, which can be removed the day they leave.
  • Two-factor authentication on every account that controls money, pages or ads. A lost phone shouldn't mean a lost business.

What changes when the business reports to your phone

You see problems in the same hour: an out-of-stock product that's still on sale, a form that sends incomplete addresses, a confirmation team that went quiet. You also see good news early, like an ad that starts bringing orders in the evening. And you stop being the bottleneck, because the team has its own tools and the owner has the full picture.

None of this is complicated technology. It's a decision to build the business around where the owner actually is.
