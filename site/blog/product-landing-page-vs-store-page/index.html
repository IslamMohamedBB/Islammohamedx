Landing page or store page? Where to send your ad traffic

Where an ad sends people matters as much as the ad itself. Here's when I send traffic to a dedicated landing page, when the store's product page is better, and how to tell which one is working.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Every ad ends in a click, and every click lands somewhere. For most stores, that somewhere is the product page. For some products, a dedicated landing page does better. The choice depends on the product, the buyer and what the page has to explain.

When a dedicated landing page wins

  • One hero product. The page can focus on a single product with nothing else to click.
  • A lot to explain. Specifications, comparisons, what's in the box, warranty and payment options need room. That's why we build product landing pages for a laptop brand we work with: each device gets its own page, in Arabic by default with English available, and its own link for ads.
  • A specific offer or audience. A page built for one campaign can repeat the exact promise of the ad.
  • Different payment options. The laptop brand's pages are built to take card and mobile-wallet payments, which suits expensive products.

When the store's product page wins

  • Many products and mix-and-match offers. Buyers want to browse, and bundles like any three for one price need the whole catalog. More in offers that convert.
  • Catalog ads, which show different products to different people and need a page for each one.
  • Repeat buyers, who already know the store and want to explore it.
  • Simplicity. One place to update prices, stock and offers, instead of keeping several pages in sync.

What a landing page needs

  • The promise of the ad, repeated at the top.
  • The price and the offer, clearly.
  • Proof: reviews, real photos and videos, specifications or ingredients.
  • Answers to the questions buyers ask, before they have to ask.
  • A short order form close to the top, and again at the bottom.
  • Speed. A landing page that loads slowly wastes the advantage of being focused. I explain the checks in site speed and conversions.
  • Orders routed straight to the team. On the laptop brand's pages, every order goes to Telegram, visible only to the team members who handle orders.

How to tell which one works

Test them against each other with the same ad, the same audience and the same budget. Judge by cost per delivered order, not just by clicks or form starts. And compare landing page views with link clicks: if many people click and few arrive, the page is too slow. Give the test enough time, too: a single week on a small budget rarely settles anything.

The product page still matters

Even when ads send people to a landing page, many buyers will visit the store too. The store's product page should be just as clear, which I describe in a perfume product page that sells. For expensive products, the page has to do even more work, as I explain in selling laptops on Meta.
