How I structure ad accounts for perfume brands

An ad account with clear jobs is easier to read, test and scale. Here's the simple structure I use for perfume brands, and why each part is there.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Perfume brands share a few traits that shape their ad accounts: many similar products, buyers who need proof before they order, frequent offers, strong seasons, and often physical branches. The account has to handle all of that without turning into fifty campaigns nobody can read.

1. Main sales campaigns: where most of the budget goes

A small number of sales campaigns with broad targeting and the best proven creatives. They get most of the budget and the most patience. In the perfume brand behind most of our sales, the biggest campaigns spent between about EGP 37,000 and EGP 81,000 each and returned between 4.17X and 4.64X. I described them in EGP 4.65M for one perfume brand.

2. A testing campaign

New creatives prove themselves here every week, with their own budget and clear rules. Winners graduate to the main campaigns. My routine is in weekly creative testing.

3. Copies for horizontal scaling

When a main campaign is already spending a lot, I often copy it with fresh creatives instead of pushing its budget higher. More in duplicating winning campaigns.

4. Retargeting

People who viewed products, added to cart, watched videos or engaged with the page. Offer-led creatives and reminders work well here, as long as the frequency stays reasonable. I explain how in smart retargeting.

5. Branch messaging, if there are branches

Location-based messaging campaigns around each branch, judged city by city, because the cost of a conversation can differ several times between cities. I showed that in the same campaign in six cities.

6. Seasonal campaigns

Ramadan, the Eids, Valentine's Day, Mother's Day and White Friday each get their own campaign, budget and creatives, and are switched off when the season ends, so they don't blur the main campaigns' numbers. I cover the seasons in Ramadan content series.

Where lookalikes and catalogs fit

A lookalike ad set can sit inside testing as one option among several. In one of our perfume accounts, a copy of a lookalike campaign returned 4.43X, which I covered in do lookalikes still work. For stores with many products, a catalog campaign can show each person the bottle they're most likely to want.

Naming rules

Name every campaign so anyone on the team can read it at a glance: the objective, the audience, the offer and the month, for example "Sales | Broad | Any 3 offer | Nov 2026". Clear names make reports faster and mistakes rarer.

Budget rules, as a starting point

  • Most of the budget in the main sales campaigns.
  • A fixed slice for testing, often somewhere around a tenth to a fifth of the budget, so new creatives are always coming.
  • A smaller amount for retargeting, sized to the audience.
  • Messaging budgets per branch, based on what each city returns.
  • Seasonal top-ups planned in advance.

I adjust these for every brand, but the shape stays the same.

What I avoid

  • One campaign per product, unless a product has its own budget and story.
  • Several campaigns chasing the same people, which makes them compete with each other.
  • Rebuilding the whole account every month. Structure should change slowly. Creatives should change fast.

If your perfume account has grown into a maze, message me on WhatsApp with a screenshot of your campaign list, and I'll tell you how I'd simplify it.
