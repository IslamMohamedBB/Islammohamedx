My editing workflow for ad videos: from raw footage to a dozen versions

The edit is where an ad is really made. Here's the workflow I use to turn one shoot into a dozen ad versions, each with a clear job in a test.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

A shoot gives you material. The edit decides whether it sells. And for ads, the goal of the edit isn't one perfect video. It's a set of versions you can test, because nobody knows in advance which opening or which length will win.

1. Start from the script and the shot list

Editing is faster when the shoot followed a plan: the hook, the problem, the product, the proof, the offer and the call to action. I explain how we write that plan in writing video ad scripts.

2. Organize before you cut

Put the footage in folders by scene and mark the best takes before opening the editor. Ten minutes of organizing saves an hour of searching later.

3. Cut the story first

Build one complete version: a strong opening, the product in use, the proof, the offer and a clear ending. Keep every shot only as long as it earns its place. The structure we use for reels is in reels that sell.

4. Then make the opening versions

The first seconds decide whether anyone sees the rest. From the same story, I make several openings: one that leads with the price or the offer, one that leads with the problem, one that leads with a reaction or a striking shot. Everything after the opening can stay the same.

5. Voice and sound

For Egyptian buyers, we use real Egyptian voices. The AI voice we tested didn't sound natural enough, so people still record our voice-overs. Keep music under the voice, and use music that's licensed for ads on the platform, such as the sound libraries Meta and TikTok provide for advertisers.

6. Captions and on-screen text

Many people watch without sound, so the key words, the price and the offer should be on screen. Keep the text short, large and inside the safe area, away from the parts of the screen the app covers with buttons and captions.

7. Lengths and endings

From one story, I usually make a short cut and a longer cut, and sometimes two endings with different calls to action. Three openings, two lengths and two endings already make twelve versions from one shoot.

8. Export for each placement

Vertical 9:16 at 1080 by 1920 for Reels, Stories and TikTok, plus square or 4:5 versions for feeds when needed. Check every export on a phone before uploading.

9. Name everything clearly

A name like product, opening, voice, length tells everyone what the video is, and makes the test results easy to read. And when you know which opening won, you know what to shoot next time. The test itself follows the routine in weekly creative testing.

Tools

We edit our ad videos in CapCut. The tool matters less than the workflow: plan, organize, cut the story, make the versions, check on a phone, name, test.

If you have footage that never became good ads, message me on WhatsApp. I'll tell you how I'd cut it.
