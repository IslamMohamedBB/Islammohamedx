Protecting your ad account: the habits that keep it safe from restrictions

For many brands, the ad account is the engine of the business. When it's restricted, sales stop the same day. Here are the habits that protect it, and what to do if it happens anyway.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Most brand owners only think about their ad account's safety after it's restricted. By then, the campaigns are off, the learning is paused and the orders have stopped. Almost everything that protects an account has to happen before that day.

1. Security first

  • Two-factor authentication for every person with access.
  • Roles, not shared logins. Everyone gets their own access in the business portfolio, and only what they need.
  • Remove access the day someone leaves, including old agencies and freelancers.
  • Ignore messages that claim to be from Meta and ask you to log in through a link. Hacked admin profiles are one of the fastest ways to lose an account.

2. Keep payments clean

  • A valid payment method, with a backup added.
  • Avoid failed payments. An unpaid balance can stop ads from running.
  • Watch the billing threshold as spend grows, so a charge doesn't fail at the worst moment.

3. Respect the advertising policies

  • No claims you can't prove, especially about health, results or "guaranteed" outcomes.
  • No personal attributes in the copy. Ads shouldn't assert or imply things about the viewer's health, finances or other personal characteristics.
  • No other brands' names or logos. For perfume stores in particular, building ads on a famous brand's trademark can get ads rejected and put the account at risk.
  • Honest prices and offers. The price in the ad should be the price on the page.

4. Landing pages that pass review

The page behind the ad is reviewed too. It should load, match what the ad promises, show how to contact the business, and include the basics such as a privacy policy and delivery and return terms.

5. Grow new accounts gradually

A brand-new account that jumps to a large daily budget overnight can attract extra review. Increase spend in steps, the same way I scale campaigns in scaling a winner without killing it.

6. Handle rejections properly

When an ad is rejected, read the reason, fix the ad or the page, and request a review. Duplicating a rejected ad again and again, unchanged, only adds more violations to the account's history.

7. Don't put everything in one place

  • More than one trusted admin on the business portfolio.
  • Business verification completed where it's available.
  • The brand owns its assets. Pages, ad accounts and pixels should sit in the brand's own business portfolio, as I explain in managing 18 brand pages.

8. Pages need protection too

Pages can be restricted for spammy behavior, including automation that posts the same reply hundreds of times. That's why CTRL Reply has a safe mode designed to keep pages within Meta's rules, which I describe in auto-replying to every comment.

If it happens anyway

Check Account Quality in Meta Business Suite to see the exact reason. Request a review once, with clear and honest information. And don't create new accounts to get around a restriction: Meta treats that as a separate violation, and it can turn one restricted account into a restricted business.
