How to brief a UGC creator (and get content you can actually use)

When UGC disappoints, the creator usually isn't the problem. The brief is. This is what goes into every brief we send, and why each part is there.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

A common story: a brand tries UGC, sends the creator two lines, the product name and "make a nice video", and is then surprised when the video is nice and doesn't sell.

A creator can't read your mind. A good brief is how you give them what they need to make something that works as an ad. Here's what I put in mine.

1. The product, in plain words

What it is, what makes it different, and the price or offer. Not the brand's mission statement. The things a buyer needs to know to decide.

2. Who it's for

One clear person: their age range, what they care about, and the problem or desire the product answers. "Young professionals who want a perfume that lasts through a long workday" is a brief. "Everyone" isn't.

3. The one message

If the viewer remembers only one thing, what should it be? Pick one. A video that tries to say five things says none of them.

4. Must-say and must-not-say

  • Must-say: the few facts that have to be in the video, such as cash on delivery, delivery time, originality or the offer.
  • Must-not-say: claims you can't back up, competitor names, prices that might change, or anything the brand isn't comfortable with.

5. Hook options

I give creators three or four possible openings and ask them to film at least two. The hook decides whether anyone watches the rest, so it's worth testing. I wrote more about openings in the first three seconds.

6. Format and style

  • Vertical 9:16, usually 20 to 45 seconds.
  • Filmed in natural light, on a phone, in a real setting that fits the audience.
  • The product in hand early.
  • On-screen text for key points, because many people watch without sound.

7. Language and dialect

Say exactly how the creator should speak. For Egyptian audiences, that usually means natural Egyptian Arabic, the way people talk to friends, not formal narration. For Gulf or Levant audiences, a creator who speaks that dialect will always sound more natural.

8. Deliverables

Be precise: how many videos, how many hook versions, raw footage or edited, with or without captions, and the file format. Clear deliverables prevent a second round of messages and a missed launch date.

9. Usage rights and timeline

Where will the content run, and for how long? Organic only, or paid ads too? Three months or a year? This affects the price and has to be agreed before filming. I explain the options in UGC usage rights explained. Add the deadline, the review process and the number of revision rounds included.

10. References

Two or three examples of videos you like, with a line on what you like about each. References save more time than any paragraph of instructions.
