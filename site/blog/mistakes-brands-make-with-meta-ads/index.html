Ten mistakes I see brands make with Meta ads, and what to do instead

When a brand brings us its ad account, the problems are rarely exotic. They're usually the same few mistakes, repeated. Here are the ten I see most, and what to do instead.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Most ad accounts that come to us aren't failing because of some hidden algorithm secret. They're failing for ordinary reasons that are easy to miss when you're also running the business. Here are the ten I run into most.

1. Boosting posts instead of building campaigns

The boost button is convenient, but it gives you far less control than a campaign built in Ads Manager. Instead: build campaigns with the objective you actually want, usually sales or messages, and use boosting only for simple reach.

2. Judging ads after a few hours

Early numbers are noisy, and buyers often order hours after they first see an ad. Instead: review on a fixed rhythm with rules agreed in advance, which I explain in when to kill an ad.

3. Changing everything, every day

Significant edits can send a campaign back into learning. An account that changes daily never settles. Instead: change one thing at a time, on a schedule, and give each change time to show its effect.

4. Too many ad sets with tiny budgets

Ten ad sets at a few hundred pounds each means ten ad sets that never collect enough data. Instead: fewer ad sets, with more creatives in each.

5. Broken tracking

This one costs the most and shows the least. In one perfume account we audited, the Pixel was reporting purchases at EGP 499 while the product sold at EGP 799. Another account reported purchases worth more than EGP 1 billion because of a broken purchase value, which I wrote about in the account that reported EGP 1 billion. Instead: test every event, check the values, and send purchases through the Conversions API as well as the Pixel, as I explain in Pixel and Conversions API for COD.

6. The same creatives for months

Every creative gets tired. Frequency climbs, clicks fall and costs rise. Instead: test new creatives every week, so there's always something ready to replace the ads that fade. My routine is in weekly creative testing.

7. Sending people to a slow or confusing page

A great ad can't save a page that takes too long to load or hides the order button. Instead: a fast product page with the price, the proof and a short order form above the fold.

8. Optimizing for the wrong result

Some brands buy messages when their buyers are ready to order on the website. Others push website purchases for products people need to ask about first. Instead: match the objective to how your buyers actually buy, as I explain in sales or messages objective.

9. Trusting Ads Manager more than the bank account

In cash on delivery, a purchase in Ads Manager is an order placed, not money collected. Instead: track confirmed and delivered orders per campaign, and judge campaigns by the cost per delivered order.

10. No offer, and a hidden price

When buyers have to ask for the price, many don't. Instead: say the price and the offer clearly. Price is the question buyers ask most often, as I explain in what buyers ask before they order.

The good news

None of these mistakes needs a bigger budget to fix. Most need discipline, a few hours of setup and someone looking at the account every couple of days.

If you recognize your account in more than two of these, message me on WhatsApp. I'll tell you which one I'd fix first.
