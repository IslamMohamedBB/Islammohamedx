Replies are content too: how to handle comments and messages on a brand page

People don't just read your posts. They read the comments under them, and your replies. A page's replies often say more about the brand than its captions do.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Most content plans stop at the post. But the post is only the beginning of the conversation. Under it, people ask about prices, compare, complain, joke and tag their friends. And everyone who scrolls through the comments reads how the brand answers.

Across the 18 brand pages we manage, Facebook counted more than 210,000 interactions between January and mid-November 2026. On top of that, our ad campaigns have started more than 101,000 conversations. That's a lot of moments where the brand either shows up or doesn't.

Every reply is public

A reply under a post isn't a private chat. It's a small piece of content that hundreds of people may read. A helpful, human reply tells every reader that the brand is real and listens. Silence, or a cold copy-paste, tells them the opposite.

Answer the question, not just "check your inbox"

When someone asks the price, "check your inbox" alone feels like a dodge. Either answer in the comment, or answer in a private message and say so publicly with something useful. Automating that first answer is what we built CTRL Reply for, as I explain in auto-replying to every comment.

Complaints: public first, then private, then public again

  1. Acknowledge publicly, calmly and quickly, without arguing.
  2. Solve it privately, where you can ask for order details.
  3. Close the loop publicly when it's solved, if the customer is happy for you to.

People don't expect a brand to be perfect. They watch how it handles problems.

One voice

The page should sound like one brand, not like five different people having five different days. Agree on the tone, the words the brand uses, the words it never uses, and how formal it should be. Then write saved replies for the questions that come up every day.

What never to do

  • Argue with a customer in public, even when they're wrong.
  • Delete criticism. Hide spam and abuse, but not honest complaints.
  • Paste the same line under every comment. People notice, and so do platforms.
  • Disappear. A page that stops answering looks closed.

Turn replies into content

The comments are a free research tool. The same questions that appear under every post become next week's videos, a pinned comment, a story highlight or a line on the product page. I listed the questions buyers ask most in what buyers ask before they order.

Measure it

Track how fast you reply, how many comments and messages get an answer, and which questions keep coming back. If the same question rises every week, the content or the product page is missing something. Speed matters as much here as in the inbox, which I explain in reply speed is a sales metric.
