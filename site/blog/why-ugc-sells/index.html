Why UGC sells better than polished ads

The ad that sells is often not the most beautiful one. It's the one that looks like a person you trust talking about something they actually use.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Some of the most expensive-looking ads I've seen performed worse than a simple phone video of someone opening a box and saying what they think. That used to surprise brand owners. Today most of them have seen it happen in their own accounts.

UGC, user-generated content, means videos and photos made by real people rather than by the brand's studio. In ads, it usually means a creator filming a natural, first-person video about a product: unboxing it, using it, comparing it, or answering the questions buyers ask.

Why it works

It looks like the feed. People scroll past ads because they recognize them. A UGC video looks like the content they chose to watch, so it earns the first three seconds more easily.

It borrows trust. A face and a voice saying "I tried this" carry more weight than a slogan. In cash-on-delivery markets, where buyers worry about quality and originality before anything else, that trust matters even more.

It answers objections out loud. A good UGC video says the things the buyer is thinking: is it original, how does it smell, how long does it last, is it worth the price. Those are the same questions that fill a brand's inbox.

It's cheaper to test. A studio shoot gives you a few polished videos. The same budget spent on creators can give you many different angles, hooks and faces, and with broad targeting, creative variety is how you find new buyers. I explain how I test in how I test creatives every week.

What makes a UGC video work

Not every phone video sells. The ones that work usually share a few things:

  • A hook in the first three seconds that names a problem or a desire.
  • The product in hand early, not at the end.
  • One clear reason to buy, not five.
  • Real reactions, in the language and dialect the buyer speaks. For Egyptian audiences, natural Egyptian Arabic beats formal narration.
  • A clear next step: how to order, cash on delivery, delivery time.

Where UGC is weaker

UGC isn't magic. It can feel cheap for luxury positioning if the creator, lighting and setting don't fit the brand. It ages fast, so you need a steady supply. And it needs clear usage rights, so a brand doesn't run a creator's face in ads without permission. I wrote about that in UGC usage rights explained.

The best accounts I run mix both: polished content that builds the brand, and UGC that does the convincing.

Why I built a platform for it

Finding good creators used to be the slowest part of the process: endless messages, unclear prices, missed deadlines, files sent over five different apps. That's why we built UGC ARABIA, a platform that connects brands with UGC creators across the Arab world. Brands browse creators, send an offer and receive the content in one place, and creators get paid for work they can be proud of.

If you've never tested UGC in your ads, start small: three creators, three angles, one product. Then let the numbers decide. And if you want help briefing your first creators, message me on WhatsApp.
