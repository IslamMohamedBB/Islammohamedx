Do lookalike audiences still work? What our perfume accounts show

Lookalike audiences used to be the go-to scaling tool on Meta. In two perfume accounts we run, they delivered 4.43X and 3.05X. Good results, but the comparison with other campaigns is the real lesson.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

A lookalike audience asks Meta to find people similar to a list you provide, usually your buyers. For years it was the standard way to scale beyond your existing customers. With broad targeting getting stronger, many media buyers now ask whether lookalikes still matter.

Here's what two of our perfume accounts show.

The two campaigns

  • A perfume brand: a lookalike-based campaign spent about EGP 40,900 and returned EGP 181,245 from 106 orders: 4.43X, about EGP 386 per order.
  • A perfume store: a lookalike campaign spent about EGP 13,100 and returned EGP 39,931 from 35 orders: 3.05X, about EGP 373 per order.

Compared with their neighbors

The useful part is the comparison inside each account.

In the perfume brand's account, the biggest sales campaigns ran between 4.17X and 4.64X. The lookalike campaign, at 4.43X, sat right in the middle of that band. It worked, but it didn't beat the other approaches.

In the perfume store's account, a sales campaign launched around the same time in June 2026 returned 4.53X, and a later one in September returned 6.33X. The lookalike, at 3.05X, was the weakest of the three.

So in our data, lookalikes are a solid option, not a secret weapon.

Why lookalikes matter less than they used to

Meta's delivery system now does much of the work lookalikes used to do. With good purchase data, broad targeting finds people similar to your buyers on its own, and it isn't limited to a fixed percentage of the population. A lookalike can still guide it, but it also narrows the pool.

When I still use them

  • Accounts with weak purchase data. A lookalike built from a good customer list gives the algorithm a head start.
  • A strong seed. Lookalikes are only as good as the list behind them. Real buyers, ideally repeat buyers, make better seeds than page engagers.
  • Testing a new angle. A lookalike ad set can be one of several tests, not the whole strategy.

How I'd test it in your account

  1. Build the seed from real buyers, the bigger and cleaner the better.
  2. Run the lookalike as one ad set next to a broad ad set, with the same creatives.
  3. Judge by cost per order over a fair spend, not by the first two days.
  4. Keep whichever wins, and keep testing creatives either way, because the creative matters more than the audience. More in broad vs interest targeting.

Give both ad sets the same budget and at least a week. Meta usually needs around 50 results in a week before an ad set leaves the learning phase, so very small tests rarely settle anything.

If your account depends on audiences built years ago and results are slipping, send me a message on WhatsApp. Sometimes the answer is fewer audiences and better ads.
