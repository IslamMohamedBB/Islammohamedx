Auto-replying to every comment: turning price questions into private conversations

Under every post that sells something, someone asks for the price. If nobody answers, that buyer is gone. Here's why we automate the first reply, and how to do it without putting the page at risk.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Scroll through the comments under any selling post in Egypt and you'll see the same words again and again: price, how much, is it available, do you deliver to my city? Every one of those comments is a buyer raising their hand in public.

Across the ad accounts we run, Meta counts more than 101,000 conversations started by our campaigns. Comments are another door into those conversations, and on many pages it's the door nobody watches.

What usually happens to a comment

  • Nobody answers, because the team is busy with the inbox.
  • Someone answers hours later with "check your messages", and the buyer has moved on.
  • Someone posts the price publicly, which works for some brands but not for those whose offers change often.

In all three cases, the interest the ad paid for leaks away.

What we built

We built CTRL Reply, our own system for Facebook and Instagram, to fix exactly this. When someone comments on a post, it can:

  • Reply to the comment publicly, so the buyer, and everyone else reading, can see the page is active.
  • Send the commenter a private message with the answer, which opens a real conversation in the inbox.
  • Follow rules set per page and per post, so each product gets the right answer.

It also has a safe mode designed to keep pages within Meta's rules, because no amount of automation is worth risking the page itself.

How Meta's private replies work

Meta lets a page send one private message in response to a comment, within seven days of the comment. If the person replies, the conversation continues like any other chat. That's why the first message matters so much: it's the one chance to start the conversation, so it has to be useful.

What to put in the private message

  • The answer to what they asked, usually the price, or the price range if there are options.
  • The key detail buyers need next, such as sizes, originality or delivery time.
  • How to order in one step: a short order form, a product link or a WhatsApp chat.
  • Delivery and payment, especially cash on delivery, which removes the biggest worry for many buyers.

I listed the questions buyers ask most in what buyers ask before they order.

Rules that keep a page safe

  • Vary the public replies. Hundreds of identical comments look like spam, to people and to platforms.
  • Keep the pace natural. Replies don't need to land in the same second as the comment.
  • Message only people who commented. Automation should answer, never cold-message.
  • Let a human take over. Once the buyer replies, the conversation needs a person, or at least saved replies that a person controls.

Measure what it does

Track the chain: comments, private messages sent, buyers who replied, and orders. If many people reply but few order, the problem is in the message or the offer. If few people reply, the first message isn't useful enough. Speed still matters after the first reply, which I explain in reply speed is a sales metric.

Automating the first reply is one of the cheapest fixes in a selling page, and one of the steps I describe in building revenue systems.
