Moving an online store off Shopify: when it makes sense, and how we did it

Shopify is a great platform, and sometimes it's still the wrong one. Here's when I'd move a store off it, what we kept and changed in a recent move, and the checklist we follow.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Shopify is one of the best e-commerce platforms in the world, and many of the stores we run are on it. But a great platform isn't automatically the right one for every store. In November 2026, we moved a perfume store's domain from Shopify to our own store template, hosted on our hosting. We're moving another perfume store off a hosted platform to the same kind of setup.

When moving off Shopify can make sense

  • The store sells almost only cash on delivery, and you want the order form to be the checkout: short, in the buyer's language, with nothing in the way.
  • Monthly costs add up. The subscription and paid apps are billed in dollars, and they grow as you add features.
  • You want full control over the pages, the speed, the data and the admin dashboard.
  • You have a team that can maintain it. A custom store needs someone to look after it. We do that for our clients.

When staying on Shopify is the better choice

  • You depend on apps and integrations that would be expensive to rebuild.
  • You don't have a developer or a partner who can maintain a custom store.
  • You sell internationally with card payments, where Shopify's checkout is hard to beat.

I compare the options in more detail in Shopify, Wuilt or custom.

What we kept and what changed in our recent move

Kept: - The domain, with its registration moved to our hosting. - The brand's existing Meta catalog and pixel, rather than starting new ones. - The domain's email.

Changed: - A short cash-on-delivery order form instead of a checkout page. - Delivery prices by region, shown before the order. - An admin dashboard for orders, products and content, which I describe in when you need a custom site and dashboard.

The migration checklist we follow

  1. Products, images and collections, checked one by one after the move.
  2. Old links redirected to the new pages, so search engines and old links don't hit errors.
  3. Tracking: the Pixel, the Conversions API and the catalog feed connected to the new store, then tested with a real order. I cover the setup in GA4 and Meta events for online stores.
  4. Google Analytics and Search Console, with the new sitemap submitted.
  5. Domain, DNS and email, moved carefully so mail keeps working.
  6. Payments and shipping, including delivery prices and couriers.
  7. Order alerts and the dashboard, so the team sees every order from day one.
  8. Test orders from a phone, through an ad link.
  9. Watch the first two weeks closely: conversion rate, order completeness and any errors.

The real question

The platform matters less than what the store does for the buyer: fast pages, clear prices, an easy order form and fast confirmation. Move when another setup does those things better for your business, not because a platform is fashionable, or unfashionable.
