28 days, 25.4K new followers, 93% organic: rebuilding a perfume page's content

We took over a perfume store's page and rebuilt its content. In the first 28 days it hit the best numbers in its history without leaning on ads. What changed, and what I watch on days like that.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

There's a moment I love in this job: the first time a page you've been working on stops behaving like it did before. With this perfume store's page, that moment came inside the first month.

We took over the page and rebuilt its content. Over the next 28 days, compared with the 28 days before we started, the numbers in Meta Business Suite looked like this:

25.4Knew followers, up 770%
4.1Mviews, 93% organic
320Kpage visits, up 598%
16K+new conversations in Messenger and DM

Interactions grew by about 1,000% to 36,000, and the best single day brought 830,000 views on 12 September 2026.

Source: Meta Business Suite, 30 August to 26 September 2026 compared with the previous 28 days. The store's name is hidden.

Read the percentages carefully

Percentages like +770% are exciting, and they're also easy to misread. They compare against the 28 days before we started, when the page was quiet. A low starting point makes any real improvement look enormous. That's why I always put the absolute numbers next to the percentages: 25,400 people chose to follow the page, and 320,000 visited it. Those are the numbers that matter for the business.

What "rebuilding the content" means

When we rebuild a page's content, we don't start with design. We start with questions: what do people already ask this brand in the inbox, what do they comment, what makes them share? For a perfume store, the answers are usually the same few things: is it original, how long does it last, what does it smell like, what's the price and is there an offer.

From there we build content around a handful of pillars, each answering one of those questions, and we post with a steady rhythm instead of in bursts. Short video carries most of the reach. Static posts and stories carry the offers and the proof. I explain how we plan a month in a content calendar for a perfume brand.

Why 93% organic matters

Only 7% of those 4.1 million views came from paid distribution. That tells me the content itself was doing the work: people watched, shared and came back. Organic reach like that is the best testing ground for ads, because a post that travels on its own is usually a strong ad creative. I wrote more about this in organic vs paid.

Followers are not the goal, conversations are

The number I care about most in this list is not the 25.4K followers. It's the 16,000+ new conversations. For a store that sells on WhatsApp and Messenger, every conversation is a potential order, and every unanswered one is money left on the table.

Growth like this only pays off if someone answers. When a page wakes up, the inbox has to be staffed, replies have to be fast, and the answers to common questions have to be ready. That's the difference between a viral month and a profitable month.

What I check on a day like 830,000 views

A spike is an opportunity with a short shelf life. Whenever a page has a day like that, I check three things quickly: is the inbox keeping up, is the offer and the order path clear for the people arriving, and is there enough stock behind the products being talked about. Then I look at what traveled and why, so we can make more of it on purpose.

The takeaway

You don't need a big ad budget to wake up a page. You need content that answers what buyers actually ask, a steady rhythm, and a team ready to turn attention into conversations and conversations into orders. If your page has gone quiet, message me on WhatsApp and tell me what you sell.
