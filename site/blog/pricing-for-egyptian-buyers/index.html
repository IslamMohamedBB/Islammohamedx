Pricing for the Egyptian online buyer: what I've learned from the ads side

Egyptian buyers compare prices in seconds, ask "how much?" before anything else and refuse surprises at the door. Pricing isn't only a finance decision. It decides how your ads perform.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

I'm not the one who sets my clients' prices. But I see, every day, how pricing decisions show up in the ads: in the comments, in the inbox, in the cost per order and in the parcels that come back. These are the lessons that keep repeating.

Show the price

The most common first message on any ad is "how much?". When an ad hides the price, the comments fill with that question, the inbox fills with it, and someone on the team has to answer it again and again. Many people never ask. They just scroll on.

Hiding the price rarely creates curiosity. It creates friction. If your price is competitive, show it proudly. If it isn't, the ad has to explain why it's worth it, and hiding the number doesn't do that.

Show the total, not just the price

A product at EGP 900 with EGP 70 of delivery is a EGP 970 product. Buyers who discover the delivery fee at the door are the ones most likely to refuse. Show delivery fees before the order button, or build them into the price and say "free delivery". Either can work. Surprises don't.

Order value decides what you can spend

The average order value sets the ceiling on what you can pay for an order. In our data, a perfume brand averages about EGP 1,450 per order, an online store about EGP 2,380, and a laptop store about EGP 4,600 across its account, with single laptop campaigns averaging far more. That's why the same cost per order can be a disaster in one store and a bargain in another. I explain the math in what an order should cost.

Raise the basket, not just the price

When margins are tight, the easiest lever is often order value rather than price: bundles, a second item at a better price, free delivery above a threshold, or a "pick any three" offer for products people buy in sets. A higher basket makes every pound of ad spend work harder. I go deeper in raising average order value without discounts.

Discounts: use them with a reason

Constant discounts train buyers to wait. A discount with a reason works better: a launch, a season, a bundle, a limited stock. And when you run an offer, put it in the first seconds of the ad, not hidden at the end.

Compare like a buyer

Before setting a price, do what your buyers will do: search for the same or similar product, check what competitors charge and what they include. You don't have to be the cheapest. You do have to be easy to justify: originality, warranty, faster delivery, better service, a bundle that makes sense.

Test prices carefully

Price tests are powerful and risky. Changing prices often confuses buyers and breaks trust. When we test, we usually test offers and bundles around the price rather than the price itself, and we judge by delivered orders and margin, not by orders submitted.

Pricing is where marketing and finance meet. If your ads get attention but people go quiet at the price, send me the product and the price on WhatsApp, and I'll tell you what the ads are telling me.
