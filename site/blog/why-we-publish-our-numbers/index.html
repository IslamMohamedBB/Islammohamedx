Why we publish our numbers, including the ones that don't flatter us

Most agencies show their best screenshot. We publish our totals, weaker accounts included, with sources and dates. Here's why, and the rules we follow.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

On our website there's a simple line: every EGP 1 we put into Meta ads came back as EGP 3 in sales, even counting the budget spent on messaging campaigns. Behind it are real totals from our ad accounts, not a single lucky campaign.

EGP 3.6M+ad spend
EGP 10.9M+attributed sales
3.0Xblended return
5,600+website orders

Source: Meta Ads Manager, lifetime data from October 2023 to November 2026 across 9 readable ad accounts. Brand names hidden.

What we include, and what we don't

  • Included: every ad account we can read in full, with all of its spend, including messaging campaigns that don't report purchase value. That's why the blended number is lower than our best campaigns.
  • Excluded: one account whose Pixel reports a broken purchase value of more than EGP 1 billion. Counting it would make our numbers absurd. I wrote about it in the account that reported EGP 1 billion.
  • Not readable: three accounts that the reporting tools can't read in full because of unsettled balances. So our totals are a floor, not a ceiling.

We publish the weaker numbers too

A results page with only peaks teaches nothing. So next to the 22X laptop campaign, we publish its copy at 13.1X. Next to the perfume brand at 3.6X, we publish a perfume chain's account at about 0.7X, because most of its spend goes to messaging campaigns for its branches, where sales happen in person. And we published a newer perfume store's account at 2.09X, with an explanation of the gap. I explain the blended number in why I report a blended 3.0X.

The rules behind every number

  1. A source and a date. Every number says where it came from and when it was pulled.
  2. Attributed is not delivered. Ads Manager counts orders placed. We say so, because in cash on delivery the difference matters.
  3. No brand names. Clients trust us with their numbers. We show categories, never names.
  4. No invented figures. If we don't have the number, we don't publish it. When we round, we say "about".
  5. Context over peaks. Ranges, averages and the story around a number, not just the best day.

Why we do it

Trust. Brand owners in Egypt have heard every promise. Real numbers, with their weaknesses, are more convincing than any promise. And numbers with a source and a date give a brand owner something to compare every other offer against.

The right clients. Publishing honest ranges filters out people who expect 20X from every campaign. The brands that stay are the ones we can actually help.

Honesty inside the team. When your numbers are public, you can't hide a bad month from yourself.

Teaching. Real numbers teach more than theory. Publishing ours, with the lessons behind them, is a way to share what they taught us.

The full story behind the totals is in EGP 10.9M in sales from our ads.
