What happens when you duplicate a winning campaign: our copies, by the numbers

When a campaign wins, the instinct is to copy it. In our accounts, copies have returned more than the original, about the same, and far less. Here's what the numbers show.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Duplicating a campaign is one of the simplest scaling moves on Meta: take what worked, copy it, and run it again, often with a new budget or alongside the original. It's also one of the most misunderstood. A copy is not a guarantee. It's a new campaign that starts with a good recipe.

Here's what copies did in some of the accounts we run.

The laptop store: a strong copy of an extraordinary original

The original sales campaign spent about EGP 23,795 and returned EGP 523,700 from 36 orders: 22X. About ten days later we duplicated it. The copy spent about EGP 15,987 and returned EGP 209,600 from 18 orders: 13.1X.

An excellent result by any normal standard, and still far below the original. The original had found a moment and an audience that the copy couldn't fully repeat. I wrote about it in the laptop campaign that returned 22X.

The perfume brand: copies that matched or beat the originals

In the perfume brand's account, several of the biggest campaigns were copies or relaunches of earlier winners:

  • A copy launched in March 2026 spent about EGP 22,017 and returned 6.05X, the highest return among the account's larger campaigns.
  • Another copy spent about EGP 37,189 and returned 4.64X.
  • A copy of a lookalike campaign spent about EGP 40,888 and returned 4.43X.

Here, the copies landed right in the account's healthy 4.2X to 4.6X band, and one beat it. I described that band in EGP 4.65M for one perfume brand.

A new perfume store: copies that beat a bigger campaign

In a newer perfume store we launched, two copies of an earlier sales campaign returned 4.07X on about EGP 15,981 (May 2026) and 3.97X on about EGP 8,979 (July 2026). Between them, a larger sales campaign in June spent about EGP 30,166 and returned 3.00X. The smaller copies did better, a reminder that a proven setup and a sensible budget often beat a bigger, fresh start.

What decides whether a copy works

  • Learning restarts. A copy enters its own learning phase. It doesn't inherit the original's history in the auction.
  • The moment changes. Competition, season and audience fatigue are different a week later.
  • Budget changes the audience. A copy with a bigger budget has to reach further than the original did.
  • The account keeps learning. In younger accounts, copies often do better simply because the Pixel has seen more buyers.

How I use copies

  • To scale horizontally when raising the original's budget would disturb it. More in scaling without killing a winner.
  • With fresh creatives added, so the copy isn't running on the same tired ads.
  • With clear rules, judging the copy on its own numbers after a fair spend, not on the original's reputation.
