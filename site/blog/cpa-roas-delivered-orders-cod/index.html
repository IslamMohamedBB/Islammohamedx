CPA, ROAS and delivered orders: the numbers that matter in cash on delivery

Ads Manager says 4X. The bank account disagrees. In cash-on-delivery stores, the gap between the two is confirmations, refusals and returns. Here's how to measure what actually matters.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Every number I publish from Ads Manager carries the same note: sales attributed by Meta. That's honest, and it matters, because in cash-on-delivery commerce an order in Ads Manager is a promise, not a payment. Between the order and the money, a lot can happen.

Three numbers, three different questions

  • Cost per purchase (CPA) in Ads Manager: how much you paid for each order placed.
  • Return on ad spend (ROAS) in Ads Manager: the value of orders placed, divided by spend.
  • Cost per delivered order: how much you paid for each order that was actually delivered and paid for. This is the one your bank account understands.

Where orders get lost

Every cash-on-delivery order passes through the same steps: placed, confirmed, shipped, delivered and paid. Some orders drop at every step. A buyer doesn't answer the confirmation call. Someone changes their mind. A courier can't find the address. A buyer refuses at the door. Each of those orders still counted in Ads Manager.

A worked example

Here's an illustration with round numbers, not a real account:

  • Ads Manager shows 100 orders from EGP 30,000 of spend, worth EGP 125,000. That's EGP 300 per order and a 4.17X return.
  • 80 orders are confirmed.
  • 68 of those are delivered and paid, worth about EGP 85,000.

The real cost per delivered order is about EGP 441, not EGP 300. The real return on collected money is about 2.83X, not 4.17X. And refused orders often cost delivery fees too, which makes the gap wider.

How to measure it properly

  1. Tag every order with its source. The campaign or ad it came from, using links with tracking parameters or the store's order source.
  2. Record what happens next. Confirmed or not, delivered, refused or returned. A simple sheet is enough. I describe one in the first 100 orders.
  3. Calculate per campaign: confirmation rate, delivery rate and cost per delivered order.
  4. Compare with your break-even. The cost per delivered order has to sit below what you can afford, which I explain in what an order should cost.

Why the campaign with the better ROAS can be the worse campaign

Two campaigns can show the same return in Ads Manager and very different results in the bank. One may bring buyers who confirm quickly and accept their parcels. The other may bring impulse orders that disappear at the confirmation call. Only delivered numbers can tell them apart.

Ads Manager still matters

Meta's numbers are the fastest signal you have. I use them to steer every day and delivered numbers to judge every week. Some stores also send the purchase event to Meta only after an order is confirmed, through the Conversions API, so the algorithm learns from serious buyers. It takes more setup, but it can improve the quality of the orders the ads bring. I cover the tracking side in Pixel and Conversions API for COD.

How to close the gap

  • Confirm fast, ideally within the hour, as I explain in confirming COD orders on WhatsApp.
  • Remove surprises: show the total with delivery before the order, and promise delivery times you can keep.
  • Fix the reasons people refuse, which I cover in reducing refused deliveries.

If your Ads Manager looks great and your cash doesn't, message me on WhatsApp with last month's orders, confirmed orders and delivered orders. The gap usually shows where to start.
