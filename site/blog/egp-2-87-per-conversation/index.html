EGP 2.87 per conversation: what a cheap message campaign taught me

One messaging campaign for a retail brand with physical branches brought 1,207 conversations at EGP 2.87 each. The account's lifetime average is EGP 13.59. Here's how I read a gap like that.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Some brands don't sell on a website. They sell in the inbox and in their branches. For those brands, the number I watch in Ads Manager isn't purchase value. It's cost per conversation: how much it costs to get one person to open a chat and start talking.

For one retail brand we run ads for, a messaging campaign launched in April 2026 spent EGP 3,458.70 and started 1,207 conversations. That's EGP 2.87 per conversation.

Put it next to the account's history

A single cheap campaign means little until you compare it. Over its lifetime, this account has spent about EGP 154,000 and collected 11,337 conversations, an average of EGP 13.59 each. A year earlier, its campaigns cost between EGP 10.82 and EGP 15.43 per conversation.

So the April campaign was close to five times cheaper than the account average. That's a real result. It's also exactly the moment to slow down and ask questions instead of celebrating.

What usually drives the cost of a conversation

From running many messaging campaigns, these are the factors I check first when the cost moves a lot:

  • Who you're targeting. Narrow audiences, like a single city around one branch, are usually more expensive than broad ones. I wrote about a clear example in the same campaign costing 4X more in another city.
  • What the ad asks for. An ad that invites a simple question ("message us for the price") gets more chats than one that asks people to decide first.
  • The creative itself. Some creatives make people curious enough to tap. Others get watched and ignored.
  • Competition and season. The same audience costs more in busy weeks when many advertisers chase it.

Cheap is not the goal

Here's the uncomfortable part. A low cost per conversation is easy to buy if you don't care who the conversations are with. Curious people who never intended to buy are cheap. Serious buyers are not.

So for messaging campaigns, I judge quality as well as cost. I read samples of the conversations: are people asking about specific products, sizes, branches and prices, or just saying "hi" and disappearing? I ask the sales team how many chats turned into visits or orders. A campaign at EGP 6 per conversation that fills the branches beats one at EGP 2 that fills the inbox with nothing.

The inbox is part of the campaign

1,207 conversations is a real workload. If replies are slow, those chats cool down and the cost per real customer quietly multiplies. Before we scale a cheap messaging campaign, I make sure the team can answer quickly, with ready answers for price, availability, branch locations and delivery. I explain why in reply speed is a sales metric.

When to use messaging campaigns at all

Messaging objectives make sense when your buyers need to talk before they buy: high-ticket products, items with many variants, brands with branches, or audiences that don't trust websites yet. If your product sells well through a simple website order, a sales campaign is usually the better choice. I compare the two in sales or messages objective.

The takeaway: EGP 2.87 is a great price for a conversation, but conversations only pay off when someone serious is on the other side and someone fast is answering. If your inbox is busy but your sales aren't, message me on WhatsApp and let's look at it together.
