Launching an online store in Egypt: the checklist we follow

We've launched stores on Shopify, on Wuilt and as fully custom websites. The platform changes, the checklist doesn't. This is what we make sure of before a store goes live.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Launching a store is exciting, and that's exactly when things get skipped. A missing shipping rate, a broken phone field, a pixel that doesn't fire. Each one costs money on day one, when you can least afford it.

This is the checklist we go through before any store we build goes live, whatever the platform.

1. The basics

  • Domain connected, with HTTPS working and one version of the address (with or without www, not both).
  • Platform chosen for the brand's stage. I explain how we choose in Shopify, Wuilt or custom.
  • Logo, colors and fonts consistent with the brand's social pages, so people recognize it when they arrive from an ad.
  • Arabic first, if most buyers read Arabic, with English where it helps.

2. Payments and cash on delivery

  • Cash on delivery enabled and clearly mentioned, because most Egyptian buyers expect it.
  • Card or wallet payments through a local provider, if the brand wants prepaid orders.
  • A short order form with name, phone, city and address. More on that in the COD order form article.

3. Shipping

  • A courier partner agreed, with rates for every governorate.
  • Shipping fees shown before the order, not discovered at the door.
  • Honest delivery times by area.

4. Product pages

  • Real photos and, where possible, short videos of the product.
  • The details buyers ask about: size, materials, specs, what's in the box, originality.
  • Price, offer and total clearly visible on mobile without scrolling forever.
  • Answers to common questions, taken from the questions the brand already gets in its inbox. For perfumes, I wrote a separate guide.

5. Trust

  • Return and exchange policy written simply, in a place people can find.
  • A WhatsApp button for questions before ordering.
  • Real reviews when the brand has them. Never invented ones.

6. Tracking

  • Meta Pixel and Conversions API with Purchase firing at order submission, the right value and currency, and no double counting.
  • Google Analytics 4 to see where visitors come from and where they leave.
  • A test order placed and checked end to end before launch.

7. Operations

  • Instant order alerts to the team's phones, so confirmation starts within minutes.
  • A confirmation process: who calls, what they say, what happens when nobody answers.
  • Stock that matches what the store says is available.

8. Speed and mobile

  • Open every key page on a phone, on mobile data. If it feels slow to you, it's slower for your buyers.
  • Compress images and remove apps or scripts the store doesn't use.

9. Launch

  • A launch offer that gives people a reason to try a new store.
  • First ads with a small test budget, several creatives and clear rules for what to pause.
  • A plan for the first 100 orders, which I break down in the first 100 orders.

None of this is complicated. All of it is easy to forget in the rush to launch. If you're about to launch a store and want a second check, send me the link on WhatsApp before you spend on ads.
