64.3M views across 18 brand pages in 2026: what the numbers say

Between January and mid-November 2026, the 18 brand pages we manage reached more than 64.3 million views. Big totals are easy to publish. Here's what's inside this one.

Islam Mohamed Abdelaziz Founder & CEO, CTRL Solutions 21 November 20263 min read

Every brand page we manage reports its numbers in Meta Business Suite. When I added up the 18 pages for 2026 so far, the totals looked like this:

64.3M+views
626K+new followers
1.57Mpage visits
955Klink clicks

Interactions across the pages added up to more than 210,000.

Meta Business Suite, Facebook page insights from 1 January to 13 November 2026, across 18 brand pages. Brand names hidden.

Where the views came from

The views aren't spread evenly. Four pages account for most of them:

  • A perfume retail chain: 24.9 million.
  • An online perfume store: 15.7 million.
  • A perfume brand: 8.3 million.
  • A laptop store: 5.8 million.
  • The other 14 pages together: 9.7 million.

Those four pages hold about 85% of all views. That's normal. Pages grow at different speeds depending on the product, the content rhythm, the budget and how long we've worked with them.

Views are not people

Views count every time content was shown. Viewers count accounts. Add up each page's viewers and you get about 15.5 million, which means each viewer saw a page's content about four times on average. And because some people follow several of these pages, the number of different people is lower still. That's why I never present views as the size of an audience. Instead, I watch each page's ratio of visits to views, because it tells me whether the content makes people want to know more about the brand.

Which numbers matter for sales

Views are the top of the funnel. They tell you how many times your content was shown, not how many people cared. The numbers closer to money are further down:

  • Page visits (1.57 million): people who actively came to check the brand.
  • Link clicks (955,000): people who took a step toward a store, a product or a chat.
  • New followers (626,000+): people who want to see more, and who become cheaper to reach later.

For a brand with a website, link clicks are the bridge to orders. For a brand with branches, visits and messages are the bridge to footfall.

Interactions are not the goal

210,000 interactions on 64 million views is a small ratio, and that's fine. A lot of today's reach comes from short video, where people watch without liking or commenting. I'd rather have a video that's watched to the end and sends people to the page than one that collects likes and nothing else.

What I take from it

  • Distribution is uneven, so judge each page by its own goal. A small page with steady messages can matter more to its business than a big page with empty views.
  • Follow the numbers down the funnel. Views, then visits, then clicks and messages, then orders.
  • Content and ads feed each other. Organic reach builds the audiences that ads later convert. I explain this in organic vs paid.
